# AI human pose, animation and motion: what is actually available

Observed: 2026-10-14 UTC (runtime clock; research updated after human clarification). Priority: recent human motion, editable 3D animation and Codex CLI/API use. Research only; no installation, paid task, account application or private upload occurred. Sources were inspected, not inference-benchmarked.

## Practical answer

There are now real text-to-3D-motion systems with released code, weights and reusable skeleton data. **NVIDIA Kimodo is the strongest first candidate in this investigation for controlled offline human motion through Codex**: CLI/Python, seeded generation, geometric constraints and BVH/NPZ exports. For interactive streaming, inspect ARDY next. For exact finger-scale vision tests, keep an explicit hand rig and camera as the geometric source; full-body motion generation does not establish detailed finger control.

The crucial distinction in X videos is the output: an MP4 of a moving person, an estimated skeleton recovered from video, and a rigged 3D motion sequence are three different deliverables. Only the last two expose 3D coordinates, and inferred coordinates are still estimates of the real performance. A frozen synthetic rig rendered from its own coordinates provides known labels for that synthetic scene.

**Finger-detail trap:** Kimodo's official output documentation says SOMA generation internally uses a reduced 30-joint skeleton while export expands it to 77 joints. SMPL-X AMASS exports include `pose_hand`, but presence of that field alone does not prove independently generated expressive finger trajectories. Inspect nonzero finger degrees of freedom and their temporal changes before using those clips to test hand articulation. BVH has motion/hierarchy, not skinned mesh/materials; GLB/FBX can carry those, but format availability alone does not certify rig quality.

## Controllable hand poses with known geometry

[MANO](https://mano.is.tuebingen.mpg.de/) is an established low-dimensional articulated hand model with shape and pose controls, learned from hand scans. Model downloads require registration and license agreement; the website offers research access, not unrestricted commercial use. It is a suitable geometric source if its terms fit this project. Renderer-side joint/vertex coordinates become exact labels for the *synthetic scene*, not proof that the hand anatomy is a perfect human model.

A simpler asset candidate is the author's [Hands Rigged](https://blendswap.com/blend/22269): left/right meshes and independent finger armature, CC0, 8.83 MB, Blender 2.7x, no textures. It is actually a hand rig, but modern Blender compatibility, bone mapping, anatomical quality and thumb controls need inspection; none was downloaded or rendered here. This avoids making a generation subscription a prerequisite. A crude procedural hand is useful for pipeline checks but may fail a pretrained hand detector because of appearance/domain shift.

[Meshy rigging documentation](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/api/rigging) explicitly supports textured humanoid assets, excludes non-humanoid assets, and discusses walking/running. That is insufficient evidence of finger articulation or a standalone-hand solution.

A very current research lead, [AnyHand](https://chen-si-cs.github.io/projects/AnyHand/), combines MANO shapes, diffusion pose priors and Blender rendering with camera/pose/depth labels. Its own beta notice says fine-tuned checkpoints are available while the full dataset and generation pipeline are still forthcoming. Do not plan on a released turnkey generator. The page also contains inconsistent dataset counts across sections; no scale claim is needed for our experiment.

## CAD and rendering are separate workflows

For dimensioned mechanical parts, Codex-authored build123d/CadQuery/OpenSCAD scripts remain a practical route: editable parameters and solid operations can be verified. [build123d interchange documentation](https://build123d.readthedocs.io/en/latest/import_export.html) documents STEP, STL and mesh export. A generated mesh converted to STEP does not recover design constraints or feature history. This route helps camera fixtures or calibration targets; it does not replace a realistic skinned hand rig.

[Blender command-line documentation](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.0/advanced/command_line/arguments.html) supports background rendering and Python execution. A shell-controlled script is enough; MCP is optional convenience. [BlenderProc](https://github.com/DLR-RM/BlenderProc) is a released GPL-3.0 procedural rendering framework supporting camera/object poses, materials, RGB/depth/normals/segmentation sequences and HDF5/COCO/BOP output. For a small fixed sequence, direct Blender Python may be less setup. Rendering can happen off-Pi, with frozen RGB frames replayed through the same Rust model path on Pi5. CPU rendering is a candidate for a tiny batch; performance was not measured here.

## Recommended next reproducible experiment

Proposed, not performed:

1. Reuse an authorized available render host/cache; inspect resources before installing anything. Choose one inspected rig with suitable rights (CC0 candidate first; MANO when authorized and appropriate).
2. Save one source scene and pinned Blender version, asset hash, units, rig-to-evaluation landmark mapping, render seed/settings and camera calibration. Map fingertip mesh landmarks explicitly: arbitrary rig bone tips need not equal the model's landmark convention.
3. Render a compact controlled sequence at the actual pipeline input size: fixed open-hand articulation; three camera distances, three yaw/pitch conditions, then one finger-flexion sweep. Vary one physical factor at a time initially. Preserve camera intrinsics/extrinsics, world and camera-space joints, projected 2D landmarks, mesh visibility/depth and timestamps alongside lossless RGB.
4. Run those exact RGB frames through the existing offline Rust inference path; report detection rate, projected landmark error where available, palm-scale-normalized error, pose/scale response, and temporal jitter. Preserve confidence and failed detections. Synthetic labels establish mathematical truth only for these renders.
5. Compare with real-frame results to expose domain shift. A synthetic failure alone cannot establish a real camera failure; a synthetic success cannot certify real-world robustness. Generative assets/backgrounds may later broaden appearance, while the rig/camera remain the label source.
